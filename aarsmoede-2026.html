Genetic Evaluation of Congenital and Early-Onset Hearing Loss: Vestibular Testing Patterns and Dysfunction

Forfattere: Andrea Lund1,2, Kristine Zøylner Swan1,2,3, Lukas Fitzgerald1, Søren Færgemann2,3,4, Uffe Birk Jensen2,4, Louise Devantier1,2

  1. Department of Oto-Rhino-Laryngology, Head and Neck Surgery, Aarhus University Hospital, Aarhus, Denmark
  2. Institute of Clinical Medicine; Aarhus University, Aarhus, Denmark
  3. Department of Molecular Medicine, Aarhus University Hospital, Aarhus, Denmark
  4. Department of Clinical Genetics, Aarhus University Hospital, Aarhus, Denmark

Background

Children with congenital sensorineural hearing loss may have concomitant vestibular dysfunction, potentially affecting balance and motor development. Several genes associated with hearing loss are also implicated in vestibular function, yet vestibular assessment is not routinely incorporated into the diagnostic work-up for hearing loss. This study aimed to characterise vestibular testing patterns and dysfunction in children undergoing audiogenetic evaluation, with genetic associations explored as a secondary analysis.

Methods

This retrospective cohort study included children undergoing genetic evaluation for congenital or early-onset hearing loss at Aarhus University Hospital between 2020 and 2025. Genetic, audiological, and vestibular data were obtained from existing clinical records. Vestibular assessment included cervical vestibular-evoked myogenic potentials (cVEMP), video head impulse testing (vHIT), and bedside head impulse testing (HIT). Balance disturbances at referral were defined as a documented history of previous falls or symptoms of balance problems or dizziness described in the referral. The present analysis represents preliminary data from the ongoing study.

Results

A total of 250 children underwent audiogenetic evaluation, of whom 101 (40.4%) underwent vestibular assessment prior to audiological intervention. Balance disturbances were recorded at referral in 25 children (10.0%), of whom 11 (44.0%) underwent vestibular assessment.

Vestibular testing was strongly associated with subsequent cochlear implantation: 81/85 (95.3%) children who received a cochlear implant were tested, compared with 20/165 (12.1%) children who did not receive a cochlear implant (p<0.001).

Among the 101 children who underwent vestibular assessment, 32 (31.7%) had at least one abnormal finding on cVEMP, vHIT, or HIT. Abnormal findings were observed in 24/86 (27.9%) children with cVEMP data, 10/71 (14.1%) with vHIT data, and 3/31 (9.7%) with HIT data. In exploratory analyses, no significant associations were identified between genetic status and abnormal vestibular findings.

Conclusion

Vestibular assessment was performed in fewer than half of children undergoing audiogenetic evaluation, and almost one-third of those tested had at least one abnormal vestibular finding. Testing was highly selective and strongly associated with cochlear implantation, highlighting the need for a more systematic approach to identifying children who may benefit from vestibular assessment and rehabilitation. Genetic associations remain exploratory and require further investigation.

Computerised dynamic posturography in Danish children: feasibility and age-related normative data

Forfattere: Bønløkke SF1,2, Owen H1, Maes L3,4, Ovesen T1,2

  1. University Clinic for Balance, Flavour and Sleep, Department of Otorhinolaryngology, Head and Neck Surgery, Gødstrup Hospital, Denmark
  2. Department of Clinical Medicine, Aarhus University, Denmark
  3. Department of Rehabilitation Sciences, Ghent University, Ghent, Belgium
  4. Department of Oto-rhino-laryngology, Ghent University Hospital, Ghent, Belgium

Background

The aim of this study is to assess the feasibility of using the Computerised Dynamic Posturography (CDP) (Virtualis, Montpellier, France) in Danish children aged 3–10 years and to establish age-related normative data for postural function in this age group.

Normal balance is essential for children’s motor development, play, school participation and physical activity. Balance depends on integration of proprioceptive, visual and vestibular input, and postural control does not reach adult-like levels until adolescence. CDP can assess functional balance, but paediatric reference values are limited. Age-related norms are needed to interpret findings and support diagnosis of dizziness, imbalance and vestibular dysfunction.

Methods

In this age-based cohort study, 55 children aged 3–10 years with no subjective hearing or balance problems were recruited from the local community. Postural function was assessed using CDP, including the Sensory Organisation Test (SOT), Motor Control Test (MCT) and Adaptation Test (ADT). Normal hearing and vestibular function were verified with otoacoustic emissions, tympanometry, video Head Impulse Test, and cervical and ocular Vestibular Evoked Myogenic Potentials.

Results

Recruitment is ongoing, and complete results are expected to be available for presentation. Participant demographics will be presented. The feasibility of using CDP in children aged 3–10 years will be evaluated. Age-related normative data for the SOT, MCT and ADT will be reported.

Conclusion

This study will provide age-related normative data for SOT, MCT and ADT using the CDP in children. These data may enable child-friendly, precise assessment of postural control, support tailored early rehabilitation and guide future research in vestibular rehabilitation in children.

Et gratis virtual reality-værktøj til undersøgelse af visuelt induceret bevægelsessyge (VIMS)

Forfattere: A Isenberg1,2, L Rogalla1,2, M Klokker1, S Serafin1,3, R Nordahl1,3, M Magnusson1,2

  1. Center for Hørelse og Balance, Afdeling for Øre-Næse-Halskirurgi og Audiologi, Rigshospitalet
  2. Hearing Systems Section, Department of Health Technology, DTU
  3. Energy Technology and Computer Science, Department of Engineering Technology and Didactics, DTU

Introduktion: VIMS kan opstå ved eksponering for visuelle bevægelsesstimuli uden tilsvarende vestibulært input. Tilstanden forekommer hyppigere hos personer med vestibulære lidelser eller migræne, og kan medføre aktivitetsbegrænsninger i visuelt komplekse omgivelser. Der mangler standardiserede metoder til undersøgelse af tilstanden i forskning og klinisk praksis.

Metode: Vi udviklede en virtual reality-applikation til kontrolleret fremkaldelse af VIMS med mulighed for præcis styring af rotationshastighed, eksponeringsvarighed og stimulationstype. For at demonstrere betydningen af dette blev 20 raske voksne randomiseret til 5-minutters rotation i Virtual Reality med identiske stimuli fraset rotationshastigheder på henholdsvis 60°/s eller 80°/s. Akutte symptomer blev vurderet hvert minut under stimulation med Fast Motion Sickness Scale.

Resultater: Applikationen fremkaldte kvalme, vertigo og okulomotorisk ubehag hos alle deltagere i varierende grad. Deltagere eksponeret for 80°/s havde højere symptombyrde målt på FMS end deltagere eksponeret for 60°/s (p < 0,05). Ingen deltagere afbrød forsøget.

Diskussion: En forøgelse af rotationshastigheden fra 60°/s til 80°/s medførte en signifikant stigning i akutte symptomer. Resultaterne understreger betydningen af præcis kontrol og rapportering af stimulusparametre. Applikationen, ”The VIMSer”, stilles frit til rådighed og kan bidrage til øget reproducerbarhed, og udvikling af fremtidig forskning og rehabilitering inden for visuel intolerance og vestibulære lidelser.
